CSV / Formula Injection

CSV is plain text, but spreadsheet apps may interpret formula-leading cells. This local preview never evaluates formulas or opens a spreadsheet.

Spreadsheet interpretation risk

CSV quoting protects the file structure, but it does not stop a spreadsheet from interpreting a formula at the start of a cell.

Why it works: a tab is placed inside the quoted cell before a formula-like prefix. This Excel-oriented mitigation must still be tested in the spreadsheet apps used by the organization.

WHAT IT IS

Text that becomes a formula

CSV injection occurs when untrusted exported values are interpreted as formulas by spreadsheet software.

OWASP: WSTG-INJT-21 (testing reference). CWE: CWE-1236. No direct OWASP Top 10 mapping is asserted.

WHY IT MATTERS

Exports can carry risk

A user-submitted value may later be opened by staff in a spreadsheet. Depending on the application, a formula may alter the sheet, mislead readers, or enable more serious actions.

DOCUMENTED CASECVE-2019-4521

IBM documented CSV injection in Cloud Pak System's Platform System Manager (CVE-2019-4521) and published a fix. This is an advisory, not proof of exploitation.

THE FIX

Neutralize each cell

  • Handle formula prefixes, including relevant whitespace and control characters.
  • Quote every cell and double embedded quotation marks.
  • Test the export in the spreadsheet programs and save/reopen workflows actually used.

There is no universal CSV sanitization strategy. The tab prefix can remain in imported data and may affect downstream processing.
